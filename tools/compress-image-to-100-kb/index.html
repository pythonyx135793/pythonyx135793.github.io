Compress an image under 100 KB

This preset targets portals that reject images above 100 KB. It measures the encoded result rather than trusting a quality slider. Processing happens in your browser; selected file contents are not uploaded.

Choose a JPG or PNG to begin.

How to use this tool safely

  1. Read the upload instructions and confirm that JPEG is accepted under a 100 KB ceiling.
  2. Select the image and run the fixed preset; no manual quality guess is required.
  3. Verify the reported bytes, then inspect the saved image rather than relying only on the preview.

Input, output, and no-upload processing

JPG and PNG inputs are supported and the output is JPEG. Transparency is replaced during JPEG encoding, and complex photographs can require reduced dimensions to pass.

What Readyvio verifies

A passing result is no larger than 100,000 bytes. That proves the Readyvio byte check only; the receiving portal may still apply format-signature, dimension, visual, or account rules.

Common 100 KB workflows

A 100 KB field often appears in job applications, university registrations, account profiles, exam forms, and general upload portals that want quick, predictable transfers. It may be paired with exact pixels or a minimum dimension, so record every requirement before you change the image.

Readyvio treats 100 KB as 100,000 bytes and reports the measured result. That explicit byte check is more dependable than a quality percentage because image detail, noise, and dimensions all affect the encoded size.

Balance readability and file size

Use the original as your source, crop away irrelevant background, and avoid repeated JPEG saves. The preset lowers quality first and may reduce dimensions only when the byte ceiling requires it. A simple, evenly lit image usually retains more visible detail than a noisy or highly textured one at the same size.

Inspect the actual download, not only the in-page preview. Look for ringing around letters, soft eyes, blocked shadows, color shifts, and clipped borders. If any required detail is lost, check the destination’s minimum dimensions or ask whether a larger limit is available.

Privacy and format checks are separate

Compression is not an exhaustive metadata-removal step. Re-encoding may remove common fields, but GPS, device, author, and editing data deserve their own inspection when privacy matters. Use the metadata viewer or removal workflow and treat its bounded verification honestly.

The output is JPEG. Transparent pixels are flattened, and a portal that accepts only PNG will reject a technically valid JPEG. Confirm format, dimensions, composition, and bytes together before submitting.

Leave room below the ceiling

A result that is a few bytes below 100,000 can still be fragile if the destination rewrites the file, applies a second upload check, or uses a different KB definition. When quality and dimensions allow, aim for a comfortable margin rather than maximising the number. Never sacrifice required detail simply to create a smaller buffer.

If the portal reports a different size after upload, compare the exact downloaded file and its encoded format. Keep the original available, record the portal’s message, and use that evidence to choose the next permitted change.

Common questions

Does 100 KB mean 100,000 or 102,400 bytes?

Readyvio uses the decimal definition: 100 KB is 100,000 bytes. Aim below the limit if the destination’s wording is unclear.

Can I keep a transparent background?

No. JPEG has no transparency. Use PNG when the destination accepts it and transparency is required.

Why do job and application portals use 100 KB?

Many forms set a compact ceiling to keep uploads quick and predictable. The limit does not tell you the required dimensions or visual composition, so read the complete field instructions.

What is a safe target below 100 KB?

There is no universal margin because portals count differently. A result comfortably below 100,000 bytes gives more room than one that is exactly on the boundary.

What if my 100 KB photo becomes too small?

Use the original, crop unnecessary background, or check whether the portal permits a different dimension or format. Never trade away required legibility just to pass a byte field.

Does 100 KB compression remove GPS location?

Do not rely on this preset for a complete privacy scrub. Re-encoding may remove common metadata, but use the metadata-removal tool and inspect its tested-field verification when location matters.

Can I compress a screenshot to 100 KB?

Yes when it is a supported JPG or PNG, but small text may blur. Crop irrelevant areas, keep required dimensions, and inspect the downloaded copy rather than only the browser preview.

Why should I avoid compressing an already compressed file?

Each JPEG save can discard more detail and add artifacts. Keep the source and create a fresh 100 KB result whenever the target or quality needs change.

Before you submit

100 KB means 100,000 bytes here. The receiving service may use a different definition, so check its published rule.
