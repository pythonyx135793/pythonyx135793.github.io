How to use this tool safely

  1. Copy the destination’s current maximum and enter it as a decimal KB value.
  2. Choose a JPG or PNG, run the compressor, and wait for the verified byte result.
  3. Download the JPEG, open it, and inspect faces, text, signatures, and document edges before submitting it elsewhere.

Input, output, and no-upload processing

Input is limited to JPG and PNG. The output is JPEG, so transparent pixels are flattened and HEIC input is not accepted. A very small ceiling may force both lower quality and smaller pixel dimensions.

What Readyvio verifies

Readyvio measures the encoded download and confirms that it is at or below the chosen decimal-KB ceiling. It does not verify a portal’s format, visual, identity-document, or minimum-dimension rules.

Where a maximum-KB limit appears

A byte ceiling is common in passport and visa portals, exam registrations, job applications, university forms, profile fields, and older email or document systems. The number is only one part of the rule: the same form may also require a particular format, exact dimensions, a minimum resolution, or a plain background.

Copy the requirement from the destination immediately before preparing the file. A remembered limit can be out of date, and a result that passes a generic compressor can still be rejected by a portal that counts bytes differently or disallows JPEG.

How to keep quality while reducing bytes

Start with the original, remove unnecessary background through a deliberate crop, and keep the smallest dimensions the destination allows. Readyvio searches JPEG quality before reducing dimensions, so it avoids throwing away pixels earlier than necessary. Busy backgrounds, sensor noise, and fine text need more bytes than a simple portrait or flat graphic.

Open the downloaded copy at normal viewing size. Check faces, signatures, small print, hair and fabric detail, sharp edges, and any border that must remain visible. A lower byte count is not a success if the result is unreadable or visually fails the receiving service’s instructions.

If the image will not fit

A very small ceiling can be impossible at the required dimensions and acceptable quality. Work from the unchanged original and try a tighter crop, a simpler permitted background, or the minimum dimensions the destination publishes. Do not keep compressing the same JPEG: repeated lossy saves can add artifacts without giving you a better starting point.

When no readable result passes, pause and check the destination’s help page or support channel. It may accept another format, a larger limit, or a different upload route. Readyvio can verify its own bytes, but only the receiving service can decide what it accepts.

Common questions

Why is the result smaller than the limit instead of exactly equal?

JPEG encoders produce discrete file sizes, not every possible byte count. Staying below the ceiling is more reliable than chasing an exact size.

Will compression remove all metadata?

Re-encoding commonly removes metadata, but this compressor does not promise an exhaustive metadata scrub. Use the dedicated removal tool when privacy verification matters.

Why do portals impose a KB limit?

Smaller uploads reduce storage, transfer time, and processing cost for the receiving service. A byte limit is separate from pixel dimensions and visual rules, so check all of them.

How many bytes are in a decimal KB?

Readyvio uses the decimal definition: 1 KB is 1,000 bytes. A 200 KB ceiling is therefore 200,000 bytes in the verification message.

What if the image will not fit under my limit?

Return to the original and try a tighter crop or smaller permitted dimensions. Do not submit an unreadable result, and check whether the destination accepts another format or a larger limit.

How do I compress a scanned signature?

Use the smallest dimensions that keep the signature legible, then choose a format the receiving service accepts. Inspect thin strokes at normal size after the byte check passes.

Why does a compressed photo look blurry?

A tight ceiling can require lower JPEG quality or fewer pixels. Start again from the original, crop unnecessary background, and reduce dimensions only as far as the destination permits.

Does the tool change my original file?

No. Processing reads a browser copy and creates a separate download. Keep the original unchanged so you can make a better result if the destination rejects the first copy.

What “maximum KB” means

This tool treats 1 KB as 1,000 bytes and verifies the finished file. It targets a file at or below your ceiling, not an exact byte count. Very small limits may require smaller dimensions or may be impossible without unacceptable quality.

Output limitations

The compressor outputs JPEG and may change color profiles or transparency. Compression does not promise removal of every metadata structure; use the dedicated metadata-removal tool when that is part of your sharing review. HEIC input is not supported.
